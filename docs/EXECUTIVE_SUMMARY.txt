================================================================================
VOLATILITY HARVESTING ON POLYMARKET: EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

RESEARCH QUESTION
─────────────────
Can retail traders profit by buying one side of Polymarket 5-minute binary 
options when it gets cheap, waiting for the opposite side to also get cheap, 
and holding both to resolution for a guaranteed payout?

HYPOTHESIS
──────────
Yes. The markets exhibit 10-30 cent swings; if you can buy both sides below $1 
total cost, you lock in guaranteed profit.

METHODOLOGY
───────────
1. Built martingale null model (20,000 synthetic markets)
2. Backtested strategy on null → established 3-5¢ cost hurdle
3. Backtested on real Polymarket data (999 markets from API)
4. Accessed HuggingFace dataset (6,742 markets with full orderbook)
5. Ran microstructure decomposition tests
6. Quantified economic value at executable prices

RESULTS
───────
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MAIN FINDING: Strategy has negative expected value  │
│ across all parameters, all market conditions         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Synthetic (null model, taker mode):
  • EV = -4.9¢/pair (mean across all parameters)
  • t-stat: -30+ (highly significant)
  • 75-82% of hedges complete successfully
  → Most trades win, but rare failures are catastrophic

Real data (999 markets, actual quotes and fees):
  • EV = -5.1¢/pair (near-identical to null)
  • t-stat: -9.7 (confirming statistical significance)
  • Performance: indistinguishable from efficient market

Mean reversion evidence:
  • Exists in BBO quote midpoints: beta = -0.064 to -0.590
  • Concentrated near 50¢ late in window: t > 40
  • But unspendable: fees exceed recoverable signal

THE MECHANISM
─────────────
1. Spread cost: 0.5¢ per side
2. Fee cost: 1.75¢ per share at 50¢ (peak of fee curve)
3. Total round-trip cost: 3-5¢
4. Recoverable reversion signal: 0.5-1.5¢
5. Result: Losing strategy

THE KEY INSIGHT
────────────────
This isn't bad luck. Polymarket's fee formula f = 0.07 × p × (1-p) was 
engineered to peak at exactly 50¢, where:
  • Volatility is highest
  • Mean reversion concentrates
  • Retail traders would most likely enter

The fee structure is a targeted defense. It doesn't ban the strategy—it just 
makes the permission too expensive.

CONFIDENCE LEVEL
─────────────────
Very high. The result is across:
  • 20,000 synthetic markets (null model)
  • 999 real markets (trade data)
  • 6,742 markets (orderbook data)
  • Multiple parameter combinations
  • Both taker and maker execution modes
  • Different fee regimes

IMPLICATIONS
─────────────
For traders:
  Before trading any strategy with dynamic fees, calculate:
    signal_magnitude ≥ (spread + fees)
  
  On Polymarket, that hurdle is 3-5¢/pair. Most intraday reversions recover less.

For researchers:
  • Bid-ask bounce creates false reversion in trade-price data
  • Always test on quote midpoints when possible
  • High statistical significance ≠ economic profitability
  
For platforms:
  Well-designed fees maintain market integrity by pricing out exploitative 
  behavior. No ban needed—just accurate pricing.

WHAT WE LEARNED ABOUT MARKETS
──────────────────────────────
1. Exploitable patterns do exist (mean reversion is real)
2. But platform design prevents retail extraction (fees as moat)
3. Professional traders operate at speeds/scales where fees matter less
4. Market efficiency emerges from fee structures, not from perfect competition

TIME & EFFORT
──────────────
• Duration: 1 week
• Markets analyzed: 6,742 resolved binary options
• Trades processed: 1.4 million
• Code lines: ~1,500 (research framework)
• Cost: ~$50 in API calls
• Outcome: Clean negative result with high confidence

ANSWER TO ORIGINAL QUESTION
─────────────────────────────
No. Retail traders cannot profitably exploit this pattern on Polymarket. 
The strategy has -3 to -13 cents of expected loss per pair across all 
parameters, with overwhelming statistical significance.

The mean reversion is real. But Polymarket's fee structure has priced the 
permission to capture it higher than its economic value.
